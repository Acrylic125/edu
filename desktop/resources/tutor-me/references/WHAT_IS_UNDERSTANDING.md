# What is understanding?

**Understanding** is how well the learner's reasoning matches the stated learning objective. A **line of reasoning** is their justification for an answer or follow-up. **Foundational concepts** are the prerequisites needed for that objective. **Carelessness** is a failed application of a concept the learner otherwise demonstrates.

## Assessment boundary

Be strict against the part's stated objective and `understoodWhen` criteria. Before asking a question or follow-up, identify the criterion it tests and the evidence still missing. Choose the smallest probe that resolves that gap. Once the learner has demonstrated every criterion, finish the check and offer the next permitted choice.

Require reasoning at the level the goal concerns. Supporting arithmetic, rounding, exact wording, and exhaustive intermediate steps are assessment requirements only when the stated goals require them. An extra demand in the tutor's own question does not expand those goals. If an earlier prompt overreached, acknowledge it and assess the relevant evidence already provided. Preserve the recorded question and answer.

For example, when the goal is tracing function arguments and return values, correctly substituting a diameter into `PI * pow(diameter / 2, 2)` and giving the correct return value can establish that trace. Requiring the learner to separately multiply `5.25 * 5.25` or round to two decimal places adds no evidence for that goal. If the goal instead requires explaining exponentiation or rounding, assess those operations directly.

## Evidence for the goal

Assess goal-relevant reasoning, not just the result:

- Is the explanation direct and intelligible?
- Are topic terms used accurately, or their meanings expressed correctly in plain language?
- Does each small, justified step follow from the previous one and the relevant concepts?

Conciseness and terminology help diagnose clarity; exact wording, brevity, or jargon are not substitutes for conceptual evidence.

For example, the objective may be: each function call has its own stack frame for call-specific locals and return information, and the frame is removed on return. Saying “different calls need their own locals and return address; their storage is released when the call ends” demonstrates the causal link. Saying “functions go on the stack, so the frame gets deleted” leaves that link unproven.

Compare the learner's actual statements across the exchange with the objective and criteria. Record what they demonstrated and what remains missing. A right answer with an invalid goal-relevant justification needs a probe. An unexplained right answer leaves reasoning unassessed only where a criterion requires that reasoning and prior answers have not already supplied it. A wrong result with a sound method may be a slip. Probe the disputed application before labelling it carelessness or a conceptual gap.

Use a targeted explanation or a meaningfully different transfer question to resolve uncertainty. Repetition with changed numbers alone is weak evidence of broader understanding. Mark only the specific demonstrated objective as understood, citing the answer/probe; keep remaining objectives open. A follow-up may strengthen or revise an earlier assessment.
