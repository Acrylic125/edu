## U.S. legal review limitations

This presentation reduces confusion; it is not a legal clearance. Nominative trademark fair use is fact-dependent and varies by federal circuit. Names may suffice to identify these tools, which can weaken an argument for using their logos. A disclaimer does not establish a defense. Copyright and service terms require separate consideration.

[This federal court decision](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-azd-2_22-cv-01950/pdf/USCOURTS-azd-2_22-cv-01950-5.pdf), page 8, discusses why copying logos can use more of a mark than reasonably necessary.

Brand policies remain relevant independently of that defense:

- [OpenAI](https://openai.com/brand/) restricts alteration, prominence, and implied endorsement; logo inquiries go to partnercomms@openai.com.
- [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/trademark-guidelines) requires specific permission and advance approval of materials. No approval has been established for EdU.
- [Google](https://partnermarketinghub.withgoogle.com/brands/google/branding-guidelines/how-to-show-googles-brand/) directs product-icon users to request permission. No approval has been established for EdU.

EdU's source implements the three provider integrations. That confirms an implementation exists, not that every subscription plan permits every use or that the providers endorse EdU. Obtain permission or U.S. trademark counsel's review before treating this use as cleared.
